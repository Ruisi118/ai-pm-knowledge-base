# Three Questions Templates

Domain-specific variations of the three key questions. Read this during Phase 2 of the rapid learning workflow.

---

## Calibration by Knowledge Level

Adjust question depth based on the user's starting point:

| Level | Q1 Adjustment | Q2 Adjustment | Q3 Adjustment |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------------|
| **Complete beginner** | Use accessible language, connect models to everyday analogies | Focus on 2-3 biggest disagreements, explain context | Start with conceptual questions, not technical |
| **Some exposure** | Use proper terminology, connect to what they already know | Include nuanced positions, not just binary debates | Mix conceptual and application questions |
| **Adjacent domain** | Bridge to their existing mental models explicitly | Highlight where their domain's assumptions break down | Include cross-domain comparison questions |

---

## Domain-Specific Q1 Variations: Core Thinking Models

### STEM Fields
"What are the 5 core theoretical frameworks that physicists/mathematicians/biologists use to reason about [topic]? For each: what class of problems does it solve, and when does it break down?"

Focus on: mathematical/theoretical models, key equations or principles, boundary conditions

### Business & Strategy
"What are the 5 core strategic frameworks that experienced practitioners use to analyze [topic]? For each: what decisions does it inform, and what does it miss?"

Focus on: decision frameworks, market models, organizational patterns

### Humanities & Social Sciences
"What are the 5 major interpretive lenses that scholars use to analyze [topic]? For each: what does it reveal, what does it obscure, and what tradition does it come from?"

Focus on: theoretical perspectives, schools of thought, methodological approaches

### Technology & Engineering
"What are the 5 core architectural patterns or design principles that experienced engineers use when building [topic]? For each: what trade-off does it optimize for, and when is it the wrong choice?"

Focus on: design patterns, system trade-offs, scalability considerations

---

## Domain-Specific Q2 Variations: Expert Disagreements

### STEM Fields
"What are the 3-5 fundamental theoretical disagreements in [field]? For each: what experimental evidence supports each side, and what would it take to resolve the debate?"

### Business & Strategy
"Where do experienced practitioners in [field] fundamentally disagree on best practices? What real-world cases support each position?"

### Humanities & Social Sciences
"What are the major interpretive or methodological debates in [field]? What are the strongest arguments on each side, and what's at stake in the disagreement?"

### Technology & Engineering
"Where do senior engineers disagree about the right approach to [topic]? What are the trade-offs each side prioritizes, and in what contexts is each right?"

---

## Domain-Specific Q3 Variations: Deep Understanding Tests

### STEM Fields
"Generate 10 questions about [topic] that require understanding mechanisms, not just memorizing facts. Include: 3 'explain why' questions, 3 'predict what happens if' questions, 2 'compare and contrast' questions, and 2 'identify the flaw in this reasoning' questions."

### Business & Strategy
"Generate 10 questions about [topic] that distinguish someone who has read the textbook from someone who has applied these concepts. Include: case-based reasoning, 'what would you do differently', and 'where does the standard framework fail' questions."

### Humanities & Social Sciences
"Generate 10 questions about [topic] that require synthesizing multiple perspectives, not just summarizing one view. Include: 'steelman the opposing view', 'apply this framework to a new case', and 'what evidence would change your position' questions."

### Technology & Engineering
"Generate 10 questions about [topic] that separate someone who has built systems from someone who has read about them. Include: debugging scenarios, 'why not use X instead', trade-off analysis, and 'what breaks at scale' questions."

## Follow-Up Question Patterns

After each of the three main questions, use these follow-up patterns to deepen understanding:

### After Q1 (Core Models)
- "You mentioned model X connects to something you know. How exactly does it map? Where does the analogy break?"
- "If you had to explain model X to a colleague in 30 seconds, what would you say?"
- "Which of these 5 models would you use first if facing a new problem in this field? Why?"

### After Q2 (Disagreements)
- "You said position A resonated more. Can you steelman position B?"
- "If new evidence emerged tomorrow that resolved this debate, which direction do you think it would go?"
- "How does this disagreement affect actual practice? Do practitioners pick sides or hedge?"

### After Q3 (Self-Assessment)
- "Looking at the questions you found hardest—what's the common thread? Is it a gap in one specific model?"
- "If you had one hour to fill the biggest gap this revealed, what would you study?"
- "Which question surprised you most? Did you think you knew more or less than you actually did?"

## Concrete Examples

### Example: Machine Learning

**Q1 Response Framework:**
1. Statistical Learning Theory (bias-variance trade-off, generalization bounds)
2. Optimization (gradient descent, loss landscapes, convergence)
3. Representation Learning (feature hierarchies, embeddings, manifold hypothesis)
4. Probabilistic Modeling (Bayesian inference, uncertainty, generative vs discriminative)
5. Computational Complexity (scalability, approximation, efficiency trade-offs)

**Q2 Key Disagreements:**
- Scaling laws vs. architectural innovation as the path to AGI
- Interpretability vs. performance trade-offs
- Bayesian vs. frequentist approaches to uncertainty
- Whether deep learning needs inductive biases or just more data

**Q3 Sample Deep Questions:**
- "Why does dropout work? Explain two different theoretical justifications and when each applies."
- "A model achieves 99% accuracy on test data but fails in production. List 5 possible causes, ordered by likelihood."

### Example: Product Management

**Q1 Response Framework:**
1. Jobs-to-be-Done (what users hire a product to do)
2. Lean Startup (build-measure-learn, validated learning)
3. Network Effects & Platform Dynamics (value creation through scale)
4. Prioritization Frameworks (RICE, opportunity scoring, cost of delay)
5. User Psychology (behavioral design, habit formation, mental models)

**Q2 Key Disagreements:**
- Data-driven vs. vision-driven product development
- Feature factory vs. outcome-oriented teams
- Build vs. buy vs. integrate decisions
- Whether PMs should be technical

**Q3 Sample Deep Questions:**
- "A feature has high usage but low satisfaction scores. What are three possible explanations, and how would you investigate each?"
- "Your A/B test shows a 5% improvement in conversion but users in the treatment group have 10% higher churn at 30 days. What do you do?"

### Example: Economics

**Q1 Response Framework:**
1. Supply & Demand (market equilibrium, price signals)
2. Marginal Analysis (marginal cost/benefit, optimization at the margin)
3. Incentive Design (moral hazard, adverse selection, mechanism design)
4. General Equilibrium (interconnected markets, second-order effects)
5. Behavioral Economics (bounded rationality, heuristics, nudges)

**Q2 Key Disagreements:**
- Keynesian vs. monetarist approaches to recession
- Efficient market hypothesis: strong, weak, or wrong
- Whether minimum wage increases reduce employment
- Optimal taxation: efficiency vs. equity

**Q3 Sample Deep Questions:**
- "Explain why rent control can simultaneously help current tenants and harm future tenants. What assumption makes this analysis controversial?"
- "A country cuts taxes and GDP grows. Does this prove the tax cut worked? What else would you need to know?"
